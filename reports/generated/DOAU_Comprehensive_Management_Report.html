EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & INSTITUTIONAL THESIS

DOAU Corporation (Dan On Foods Group) operates as a key regional supplier and wholesale distributor of processed nuts, agricultural commodities, and processing machinery. Over the 10-month period ending October 31, 2026, DOAU achieved cumulative net revenues of $10.08M. While the business encountered gross margin compression earlier in Q2 due to commodity market shifts, October 2026 results exhibited a decisive top-line rebound with monthly revenues rising to $1,359k.

Resilient core demand across contracted wholesale relationships

The top-line expansion in October was anchored by key institutional distribution partners (Falcon Trading, Wal-Mart, Dollarama). Despite inflationary headwinds, DOAU's order volumes demonstrated structural durability, proving the strategic necessity of its warehousing and 48-hour regional fulfillment capabilities.

... disciplined OPEX containment providing runway for turnaround

Management enacted tight administrative expense controls throughout Q3, consolidating monthly overhead at $109k. This discipline prevented further operational cash leakage while contract pricing realignments take effect across core SKUs.

MANAGEMENT STRATEGIC ACTION PLANS

Action Plan 1: Margin Rehabilitation & Surcharges
Eliminate off-invoice volume discounts. Implement freight surcharges for regional deliveries outside the primary California transit radius, establishing a 4.5% target gross margin threshold for 2027.

Action Plan 2: Working Capital & Cash Acceleration
Form an aggressive credit collection taskforce to convert 100% of the $844k 1–30 day receivables into cash within 25 days, reducing reliance on revolving borrowing facilities.

Action Plan 3: Inventory SKU Rationalization
Liquidate slow-moving secondary SKUs (representing <5% of volume but >$400k in carrying value) to release capital and simplify warehouse storage fees.

SALES REPORT: YTD PERFORMANCE UPDATE

DOAU registered total cumulative sales of $10.08M across the first 10 months of 2026. A detailed examination of monthly run-rates reveals three distinct operational phases across the reporting cycle:

1. Strong Q1 Baseline & High-Volume Delivery
  • Robust Order Realization: The year opened with strong demand in January ($1,015k), driven by holiday restocking and high wholesale throughput.
  • Seasonal Baseline Normalization: February ($702k) and March ($685k) stabilized at normal quarterly run-rates across key supermarket chains.
  • Supply Chain Availability: Early inventory availability permitted aggressive customer commitments across both third-party accounts and intercompany transfers.
2. Summer Re-balancing & Trough Cycle (May - July)
  • Transient Contraction: Order intake softened in May ($713k) and July ($1,253k nominal but volume compressed) due to global shipping container delays.
  • Harvest Timing Variations: Shifts in California raw almond harvest timing delayed bulk customer procurement cycles.
  • Credit Risk Management: Tightened credit limits among tier-2 wholesale clients temporarily deferred non-essential shipments.
3. Q3/Q4 Accelerating Recovery (August - October)
  • Aggressive Resurgence: Monthly revenue expanded from $977k in August to $1,359k in October (+39.7% above summer low).
  • Holiday Stocking Acceleration: Strong pre-holiday retail demand for baking ingredients, dried cherries, and snacking mixed nuts.
  • Operational Momentum: Confirms underlying consumer resilience and sets a constructive foundation for Q4 cash flow generation.
COMMERCIAL PERFORMANCE COMMENTARY & GROWTH DRIVERS

Customer Re-order Cadence: Primary institutional clients displayed consistent 30-day replenishment orders, reflecting strong retail sell-through rates.

Pricing Realization: While list pricing was maintained, high wholesale demand enabled the gradual removal of promotional volume rebates toward the end of October.

INVENTORY ANALYSIS & WAREHOUSE OPTIMIZATION

With total physical inventory standing at $3.61M, inventory constitutes 57.0% of DOAU's total current assets. Managing warehouse turnover and carrying costs is the single most vital operational determinant of liquidity.

Asset Concentration & Storage Dynamics

Inventory holdings are concentrated in high-value almond kernels, raw pistachios, and dried fruit drums:
• Cold-Storage Commodities: Almonds and dried cherries require temperature-controlled warehouse space in California, incurring monthly holding and refrigeration utility charges.
• Packaging Supplies & Seasonings: Constitute secondary inventory buffers necessary to support rapid turnaround for specialized packaging customer orders.

Inventory Aging & Days Sales of Inventory (DSI)

Calculated DSI currently averages approximately 95 operating days, elevated relative to wholesale industry benchmarks of 60–70 days. This lengthening reflects seasonal crop reception prior to peak fourth-quarter distribution runs.

INVENTORY ACTION & LIQUIDATION STRATEGY

1. SKU De-proliferation: Eliminating 120 slow-moving SKUs with zero movement over the past 90 days.
2. Just-In-Time Procurement: Transitioning non-core packaging materials to consignment terms, shifting carrying liability back to primary suppliers.
3. Targeted Capital Release: Targeted liquidation of $500k in excess stock by end of Q4 to reduce carrying costs and enhance cash reserves.
